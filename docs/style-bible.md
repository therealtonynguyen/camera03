# Style Bible

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · Companion to [`feature-concept.md`](feature-concept.md)

This document defines the house visual style for the feature and for OFF CAMERA shorts made in that style. When a frame, prompt or edit raises a question about how something should look, the answer belongs here.

---

## North Star

> **How detailed a character is drawn shows how much the story sees them.**

Every choice about color, line, detail, motion and light serves that one idea. The protagonist starts as an extra and becomes the main character, and the audience *watches* that happen in how he is drawn.

---

## Principles

1. **One world, one look.** Every character, location and crossover is drawn in this style. Nothing is imported in its original look.
2. **Detail is earned.** Visual detail follows how much the story pays attention to someone, not how important they are in the world.
3. **Cameras have a voice.** Device footage is a stylized translation of that camera, not a realistic copy of it.
4. **Light means something.** Warm light is used deliberately, sparingly, and never explained.
5. **Restraint over spectacle.** When in doubt, leave it out. The big events can be loud; the style itself stays quiet.

---

## Influences and Boundaries

**Learn from:**

- *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* and *Across the Spider-Verse*: frame rate as character, a mix of 2D and 3D, a style that serves the story
- *KPop Demon Hunters*: a bold, graphic house look that stays consistent across action and quiet moments
- *Arcane*: painterly texture on 3D forms
- *Klaus*: 2D-style lighting and volume on hand-drawn characters
- Stained glass and printmaking: lead lines, flat color fields, light carried by color

**Don't copy:**

- Spider-Verse's halftone dots, offset printing errors or comic captions. Those belong to that film.
- Any recognizable Disney or Pixar rendering look
- The original designs of crossover characters (see *Crossover Redesign*)

The goal is a look that is clearly *ours* in one frame.

## The Fidelity Scale

How much detail something is drawn with is measured on a five-level scale. Every character in every shot has a fidelity level.

| Level | Name | Palette | Line | Detail | Shading | Frame rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F0** | Silhouette | 1–2 flat tones, near-gray | None or a single outline | Shape only, no face | None | On 4s (6 fps) |
| **F1** | Extra | Muted, ~20% saturation, cool | Thin, uniform | Simple features, no texture | Single flat shadow | On 3s (8 fps) |
| **F2** | Supporting | Moderate, ~50% saturation | Varied weight | Clothing folds, hair shapes | Two-tone shading | On 2s (12 fps) |
| **F3** | Featured | Full saturation | Expressive, tapered | Texture, small details | Painterly gradients | Mixed 2s and 1s |
| **F4** | Main character | Full saturation plus a warm accent | Confident, alive | Full texture, fine detail | Full painterly lighting with rim light | On 1s (24 fps) |

**Rules:**

- Fidelity belongs to the *character*, not the shot. A close-up of an F1 extra is still F1.
- Blockbuster events and their heroes are always **F4**. They are the main characters of *their* stories.
- Environments match the fidelity of whoever the scene is about.
- A character's fidelity changes only at story beats, never randomly from shot to shot.

## Character Fidelity Arc

### The protagonist

| Point in story | Level | Visual notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opening (watching monitors) | F1 | Lit mostly by the blue glow of the monitors |
| Act 1 (detour, first porch visit) | F1 | Blends into crowds. Easy to miss in wide shots. |
| End of Act 1 (realizes it keeps happening) | F1 → F2 | First change. Subtle: slightly warmer skin tones, his line gets some weight. |
| Act 2 (dates, the pattern) | F2 | Climbs toward F3 in moments with her, drops back when he retreats |
| Low point (decides to stay in the background) | F2 → F1 | He visibly loses detail. The one time fidelity *drops*. |
| Act 3 (goes through the chaos) | F2 → F3 | Rises steadily with each choice to keep going |
| Climax (at the door) | **F4** | First time at full fidelity. The moment the door opens. |

### The best friend

She mirrors him. In the first half she is **F2** whenever she is with him and **F1** when she appears in the background of blockbuster events (which the audience only notices in the credits). At the climax she reaches **F4** alongside him.

### The stranger

**Always F4, even in the background.** In any crowd, the stranger is the one figure drawn in full detail, at 24 fps, often touched by warm light. Most viewers won't consciously notice this until the credits. It should be possible to spot on a second viewing.

### Background extras

F0–F1 throughout. In the credits, each featured extra gets a short moment at F3–F4.

## Color

### Palette scripts

| Palette | Use | Feel | Starting values |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Background** | His world, Act 1 | Cool, desaturated, slate and gray-blue | `#5B6573` `#8A939E` `#C4C9CF` |
| **Monitor glow** | Security office, screens | Cold blue-cyan | `#3A6E8F` `#7FB8D4` |
| **Blockbuster** | Crossover events | Saturated, high contrast, their own color identities | Defined per event |
| **Her** | Scenes with the best friend | Gentle warm neutrals, which grow warmer through the film | `#C9A88A` `#E8D5C0` |
| **Grace light** | See *Light* | Warm amber-gold | `#F2B45A` `#FFD58A` |

### Rules

- His world warms gradually across the film. The last shot is the warmest frame of the movie.
- Blockbuster palettes stay contained. They don't spill into his color script.
- No pure black and no pure white. Shadows lean blue in Act 1 and warm by Act 3.

---

## Line and Texture

- **Line:** a hand-drawn-feeling contour over 3D forms. Weight increases with fidelity level (see the scale).
- **Brushwork:** painterly texture on surfaces. Visible but soft, never noisy.
- **Edges:** at F0–F1, shapes may lose their outlines and merge into the background. At F4, edges are crisp and lit.
- **Motion lines and smears:** allowed for blockbuster action. Kept minimal around the protagonist until Act 3.

---

## Motion and Frame Rate

| Rate | Feel | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| On 4s (6 fps) | Stilted, peripheral | Silhouettes and distant crowds |
| On 3s (8 fps) | Clipped, slightly lifeless | The protagonist in Act 1, extras |
| On 2s (12 fps) | Classic hand-drawn | Supporting characters, his Act 2 |
| On 1s (24 fps) | Fully alive, fluid | Blockbuster heroes, the stranger, the climax |

- Mixed frame rates in one shot are intended. An 8 fps protagonist standing next to a 24 fps superhero is the point.
- The camera itself always moves smoothly at 24 fps, even when characters are on 3s.
- **The climax:** as the door opens, he goes to 1s on a single frame. The audience should *feel* the change before they understand it.

## Light

### Grace light

A warm amber-gold light that finds the protagonist at a handful of key moments.

**Rules:**

- **Motivated, but not quite.** It always has a plausible source (a window, a streetlight, a gap in the clouds), but it is a touch warmer and more focused than that source should be.
- **Never commented on.** No character reacts to it.
- **Rationed.** No more than 6–8 times in the whole film. Each use should feel earned.
- **Often on the stranger.** When the stranger appears, the light is near them.

**Planned moments (draft):**

1. Act 1: the first porch visit, as he turns to leave (he doesn't see it)
2. Act 1: the detour, the moment the street he would have taken is hit
3. Act 2: across the table on their best night (the food truck)
4. Low point: it's there, and he walks out of it
5. Act 3: as he decides to go through the chaos
6. Climax: on the porch as the door opens

### Stained-glass mode

At the climax, and possibly once in Act 3, the style moves toward stained glass:

- Contour lines thicken into dark lead lines
- Color areas flatten into clean fields
- Grace light passes *through* the color, so the fields glow from behind
- It lasts seconds, not minutes. It shouldn't feel like a separate sequence.

## Camera Footage Mode

Device footage stays in the house style. It translates the camera's look into graphic language rather than imitating video realistically.

| Real artifact | House style translation |
|---|---|
| Compression blocks | Visible painted pixel blocks in flat color areas |
| Sensor noise | A fine, even brush-grain texture |
| Scanlines / interlacing | Thin drawn horizontal lines |
| IR night vision | A two-tone green-gray or gray print look with a soft center hotspot |
| Fisheye distortion | Real lens distortion, kept |
| Low frame rate | On 4s or 3s for every character *except* F4 characters |
| Timestamps and UI | Clean monospace type, generic invented UI, never real brands |

**Rule:** in camera footage, the F4 characters still move at 24 fps even though the device is choppy. In the doorbell climax, he and she move fluidly inside a stuttering camera image.

Device specifics (mount height, lens, overlays) come from [`visual-language.md`](visual-language.md).

## Crossover Redesign

Every blockbuster character and location is redrawn in the house style. Under the recommended rights path, they are **original stand-ins** (see `feature-concept.md` → *Rights*).

**Redesign rules:**

- **Keep the archetype, change the design.** A retro super family should be recognizable as a type, not as specific characters.
- **No trademark details:** signature costumes, logos, emblems, exact color schemes or famous silhouettes.
- **Each event gets its own palette** inside the house style, so they stay distinct from each other.
- **Always F4.** They are the leads of their own stories.
- **Test:** if a lawyer would point at the frame and name the original character, redesign it.

---

## Environments

- Environments match the fidelity of whoever the scene is about.
- **His apartment and the security office:** F1–F2, cool, a bit sparse. They warm up over the film.
- **Her porch:** the most important location. Designed once and kept consistent. It must work in normal coverage *and* in doorbell camera framing. It is the same porch geometry as *CAM 03 / 001*, redesigned in this style.
- **The city:** F1 in his everyday routes and F4 wherever a blockbuster event is happening.

## AI Production Pipeline

Keeping a custom style consistent is the hardest production problem. Video models drift back to their own default looks.

### Style lock

1. **Reference sheet:** create 10–20 approved frames covering every fidelity level, the palettes, camera footage mode and stained-glass mode.
2. **Character sheets:** the protagonist at every fidelity level (F1–F4), front, three-quarter and profile. The same for the best friend and the stranger.
3. **Style model:** train a custom style model (for example a LoRA) on the reference sheet where the tools support it. Keep separate fidelity references for F1 and F4.
4. **Version everything:** keep approved references in `style/` with version numbers. Never generate from an unapproved reference.

### Workflow

Same frame-first rule as the OFF CAMERA workflow:

```
Approved style reference → Static frame at the target fidelity → Image-to-video → Frame-rate pass in post → Grade and overlays
```

- **Frame rate in post:** generate at the model's native rate, then hold frames in the edit to get on-2s or on-3s motion. Mask F4 characters to keep them at 24 fps where needed.
- **Fidelity in the image stage:** set fidelity in the static frame. Video prompts should never ask for changes to the style.

### Prompt building blocks

Draft language to refine through testing:

```
HOUSE STYLE
painterly 3D animation with hand-drawn contour lines, soft visible brushwork,
graphic shapes, stylized lighting, no photorealism

F1 (extra)
muted desaturated cool palette, thin uniform outline, simplified features,
flat single shadow, minimal texture

F4 (main character)
full saturation, confident tapered linework, rich painterly texture,
warm rim light, fine detail

GRACE LIGHT
warm amber-gold light from [source], slightly warmer and more focused than
natural, falling on [subject]

CAMERA MODE
stylized [device] footage in illustrated form, painted pixel blocks, fine
brush grain, drawn scanlines, monospace timestamp
```

### Consistency check (each generated shot)

- [ ] Fidelity level matches the character's point in the story
- [ ] Palette matches the scene's palette script
- [ ] No drift toward a recognizable studio look
- [ ] No original crossover designs visible
- [ ] Grace light only where planned
- [ ] Camera footage reads as illustrated, not realistic video

## Do and Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Let the drawing style show the story | Explain fidelity changes in dialogue |
| Keep the protagonist easy to miss in Act 1 | Make him look "ugly." F1 is simple, not bad. |
| Ration grace light | Use warm light as a general mood lighting |
| Redesign every crossover | Import any original character design |
| Keep camera footage illustrated | Drop in realistic video textures |
| Mix frame rates on purpose | Let frame rates drift between shots |
